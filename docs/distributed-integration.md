# Distributed Git integration

A client integrates an explicitly named source into an explicitly named remote branch. The service owns an exclusive lease for that target. The service never executes Git. It validates the actor, execution generations, complete group membership, formal deliveries and closed review targets under the board transaction locks. The client verifies the registered common remote and actual ancestry.

## Operations and authority

`kander coordinator integrate <absolute-integration.json>` accepts UTF-8 JSON up to 64 KiB. Required fields are `operation_id`, `purpose`, `target_ref`, `commit`, and `decision`. `via_commit` is optional and only valid with purpose `deliver`. Unknown fields and trailing JSON are rejected. `commit` and `via_commit` are complete SHAs and `target_ref` is a complete `refs/heads/…` name.

| Purpose | Scope | Authority and effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `initialize` | `group_id` | Current private coordinator; create or adopt the group's exact anchor at `refs/heads/group/<group-id>` |
| `assemble` | `group_id`, `contributors` | Current private coordinator; fast-forward that group branch with the named current review deliveries |
| `deliver` | `task_id` or `group_id` | Current task holder or private coordinator; bring the current closed review target into an explicit final target, by one of the four containments below |

Group requests include the complete topology, even when only some members contribute to an assembly. Each contribution binds its current card revision, task generation and formal immutable delivery. An audited excluded member does not contribute to final delivery. In a group `deliver`, a `done/` member stays in the topology without a delivery commit and still needs its closed review target to equal `commit`, so a member taken over after an earlier group receipt can integrate again (`contained`) for its new generation; the client refuses a group `deliver` without any contributor before it requests the target lease, and the service refuses one that bypasses the client. A standalone `deliver` keeps requiring a current delivery of its card. A public coordinator session label grants no authority. The target lease does not grant ownership of any task.

The target identity is `i-<SHA256(ref)>`. Its private token, execution, generation and resource revision are independent of task claim generations and dispatch epochs. Renewals change neither card content nor checkpoint versions. An expired target that nobody claimed again reads as `lapsed`: its owner keeps renewing, pushing and confirming at the same generation, in every phase including `pushing`, because the actor check takes the actor's standing (the `standing` event of its claim or group kernel) instead of its lease clock. Acquiring another generation requires a lapsed or ended target, a fresh inspected target record and an explicit decision; the old owner's later requests are answered `taken_over`. Every phase change and its precise operation receipt share one redo transaction.

## Standalone cards

A task group member reaches `review/` through its own dispatch. A standalone card (no task group) has no dispatch, so its executing agent publishes the delivery itself by moving into `review/`; `rules/snippets/execute-single.md` "Distributed Mode" holds the rule that this section backs.

Prerequisites:

- The review plan is sealed with every batch closed, and its last batch has a Git target `C`, a complete SHA. The agent verifies the integration authorization and every gate before it moves the card.
- `TASK_BRANCH` is filled in; the move into `review/` requires it.
- The move runs on the host that holds the card's execution credential. The client publishes the delivery from the worktree recorded in that credential (the current directory when none is recorded) and refuses the move on another host with `remote.delivery_holder_required`.
- The task branch is not rebased after the plan closed. A rebased commit has another SHA than `C`, and `done` compares the receipt with the closed target. Only a client-reported conflict calls for a merge and `via_commit`, as above.

Steps:

1. `kander move <task-id> review --delivery-commit <C>` publishes `C` as the immutable delivery of the current claim generation and moves the card from `working/` into `review/`. The agent then continues at once; nothing is dispatched back.
2. `kander coordinator integrate` with purpose `deliver`, `task_id`, and `commit` equal to `C`. The client requires the card in `review/` with a standing claim and a non-baseline delivery of the current generation and execution. A card in `working/` fails with `remote.integration_delivery_required`, which names the card, its state, and the move command with the requested commit. The service repeats the checks and refuses a member whose review is not closed with the board reason, prefixed by `member <task-id>: `, and a commit that differs from the closed review target with a message that names the member, the closed target, and the requested commit.
3. After the final receipt exists, `kander move <task-id> done --result completed` runs from `review/`. A failure after the move into `review/` leaves the card in `review/` with the pending items recorded.

A plain `kander move <task-id> working`, with no `--owner` and no dispatch binding, returns the card from `review/` to `working/` for the same holder; the claim generation and the execution stay. A later `move review --delivery-commit` publishes the next delivery. The return does not reopen the review: a closed batch cannot advance and a sealed plan takes no further batch, so a code change that needs review belongs to a new card with its own plan.

A card whose review plan is the explicit N/A plan has no `C`. It never moves into `review/`: a published delivery makes completion require an integration, which that plan cannot provide. It completes from `working/`.

## Source publication and target update

A newly composed merge may exist only in the coordinator's clone. After obtaining the target lease, the client publishes it at `refs/heads/kander/<project>/integrations/<execution>/<SHA>`. This source ref never changes until the completion cleanup deletes it; it has its own saved intent and an absent-ref compare-and-swap. It does not reuse a member's delivery ref. Recovery observes the exact ref before attempting publication again.

Before claiming, the client checks each contributor's immutable delivery ref in the common remote, including when its commit is already cached. It verifies source ancestry locally. Before changing the target it commits a `pushing` intent and renews its authority. The push uses the exact observed target SHA as a force-with-lease condition. The client pushes only a commit that fast-forwards the observed target. A source that the target already holds, in either of the ways below, uses a read-only confirmation and cannot obtain push intent.

## Containment of a delivery

A `deliver` request always carries the closed review target `C` as `commit`. The review target is what the review plan closed; the integration source ref, the receipt checks of `done` and the wrap-up evidence all keep using `C`. The target branch head observed at the start of the operation is `T`. The client decides how `C` reaches the target, in this order, and records the decision as `containment` in the request:

1. With a `via_commit` `V` in the input, only the `via` rule applies. `V` must be served by the common remote, which the client proves by a fetch negotiation through the clone's own `origin` (a plain fetch would succeed from the clone's own objects). Negotiation needs Git protocol version 2; when it fails, the client fetches that one commit shallowly into an empty scratch repository from the remote URL, resolved against the work tree when it is a relative path. `V` must descend from `C`; a `V` that is not served by the remote, or that does not descend from `C`, is refused before any lease is claimed. To put `V` on the common remote, push the task branch under its own name, `kander-task/<project-id>/<task-id>/g<generation>`; a card that still records the old name `kander/<project-id>/<task-id>/g<generation>` pushes it with `git push origin <TASK_BRANCH>:refs/heads/kander-task/<TASK_BRANCH without the leading kander/>`. Task branches live below `refs/heads/kander-task/`, and below `refs/heads/kander/` there are only evidence refs. Then:
   - `V` descends from `T` (or equals it): used as given. When `V` equals `T`, nothing is pushed and the confirmation is read-only; otherwise the client pushes `V` to the target.
   - `T` contains `V` (`V` is a proper ancestor of `T`, because the target moved on after `V` was made): nothing is pushed. The receipt records `via_commit` = `T`, `already_contained` = true, and `via_source` = `V`.
   - `T` and `V` have diverged: the client merges them as in rule 5 (first parent `T`, second parent `V`) and pushes that merge `M`. The receipt records `via_commit` = `M`, `via_auto_merge` = true, and `via_source` = `V`.

   Changing `via_commit` needs a new `operation_id`.
2. `contained`: `C` is an ancestor of `T` (or equal). Nothing is pushed.
3. `fast_forward`: `T` is an ancestor of `C`. The client pushes `C`.
4. `replayed`: the target holds a run of consecutive commits that repeats the review range `B..C` commit by commit. `B` is the first batch base of the closed review plan, taken from the dispatch review range. Candidates are the single-parent commits of `B..T`, wherever they entered the target: on its first-parent chain or through a merge, and also after commits of the range that the target holds under their own SHA. A run is a chain in which each commit has the previous one as its parent; when several children have the same patch, every branch is tried. It has the same number of commits as the range, and each pair, in order, has the same patch. A patch is the full binary diff of a commit against its first parent. Only the `index` lines and the hunk offsets are ignored, which is the normalization of the rebased wrap-up evidence. A review range that contains a merge commit, an empty range, more than 256 review commits, or more than 4096 commits in `B..T` is never judged equal. Nothing is pushed.
5. Otherwise the client merges `T` and `C` itself. The merge `M` is built from objects only: `git merge-tree --write-tree T C` produces the tree, and `git commit-tree` writes `M` with first parent `T` and second parent `C`, using Git's default merge and no strategy options. The work tree, the index, `HEAD` and every local branch stay as they are. `M` is kept under the private ref `refs/kander/auto-merge/<execution-id>`, never pushed, until the attempt completes or is given up; a retry of the same `operation_id` before the push reuses the commit that ref names, and when the ref is lost and the target does not hold `M`, the client gives up that attempt and starts a new one. The commit message is a fixed English text naming the target branch, `C`, the `operation_id` and, for a merge with a via commit, `V`. Author and committer come from the Git identity configured for the repository (`user.useConfigOnly=true`); there is no guess from the host name. The client pushes `M` to the target with the usual lease on `T`. `M` need not be on the common remote beforehand; the push publishes it. The receipt records `containment` = `via`, `via_commit` = `M`, `already_contained` = false and `via_auto_merge` = true.

The merge is refused, with nothing pushed and no lease claimed, in three cases. A conflict exists: only exit status 1 of `merge-tree` with a tree object name on its first output line counts, and the conflicting paths come from its NUL-separated `--name-only` output; the error lists up to ten of them with their total and says to merge the target into your own branch, resolve the conflicts, re-verify, push the result to the common remote, and integrate with a `via_commit`. The local Git is older than 2.38, where `--write-tree` is an unknown option (exit status 129): the error names the version. No Git identity is configured: the error says so. Any other non-zero exit of `merge-tree` is an ordinary Git error. The rules never resolve a conflict automatically.

Every contributing member's latest delivery `D` must be in `C` (an ancestor of `C`, edge `D → C`) or in the target that the integration leaves (edge `D → F`). `F` is `C` for `fast_forward`, `V` (the `via_commit` of the request, which is `M` for a merge the client made) for `via`, and `T` otherwise. The service requires exactly one of the two edges per contributing member (an edge that is also a containment edge counts, so a delivery equal to `C` under `contained` is served by `C → T` and the client adds no second edge) and then requires the complete edge set to equal what the containment needs: `T → C` for `fast_forward`, `C → T` for `contained`, `T → V` and `C → V` for `via` (and one more edge, `via_source → via_commit`, when the request carries `via_source`), and `R → T` for `replayed`, where `R` is the last replayed commit. A request without `containment`, as an older client sends it, keeps the earlier rule: the edge `D → C` for every contributing member. The client proves every edge with Git. The service never executes Git, so this proof stays with the client.

The receipt records the containment. `containment` is one of `fast_forward`, `contained`, `via` and `replayed`. A `via` receipt also records `via_commit`; the content of `C..via_commit` is the part that no review saw. Two optional fields describe a `via` receipt further. `via_auto_merge` (true) marks `via_commit` as a merge the client made. With `via_auto_merge` and without `via_source`, `C..M` is the commits the target already held plus one conflict-free merge, with nothing written by hand. `via_source` records the caller's `V` when the merge was made from it, or when the target already contained it; then `C..V` is part of what no review saw, for example a conflict an agent resolved by hand. kander does not build or test `M`. The service accepts `via_auto_merge` only with `containment` `via`, `already_contained` false and purpose `deliver`, and `via_source` only with `containment` `via`, different from `via_commit`, and together with `via_auto_merge` or `already_contained`; the edge set must then hold exactly the extra `via_source → via_commit` edge. Both fields are omitted when empty, so a historical request or receipt keeps its bytes. A service that predates them decodes requests strictly and refuses one that carries either, so upgrade the service before any client. A `replayed` receipt records `replay` with `review_base`, `review_target`, `replay_base` (the parent of the first replayed commit) and `replay_target`. `already_contained` keeps its meaning: it is true for `contained`, for `replayed`, and for `via` when `V` equals `T`. A receipt without `containment` is a historical record: it reads as `contained` when `already_contained` is set and as `fast_forward` otherwise, and its bytes stay unchanged.

The client always reads the real remote after a push, including a nonzero exit. A command exit alone is not publication evidence. Confirmation requires fresh service-clock observation material and the verified ancestry of the confirm commit in the observed target: `C` for every containment except `replayed`, which uses `R`. The service atomically publishes the integrated result and releases the target lease.

## A target that moves

Anyone may push the target branch while an integration runs, and the integration tolerates it. When the client sees that the target is no longer the one an attempt observed, and the attempt has pushed nothing, it releases the target lease, deletes the attempt's credential and its private merge ref, records why it gave the attempt up (`target_moved`), and starts a new attempt from a fresh observation; the new attempt decides the containment again, so a moved target that now holds `C` ends as `contained` without a push. This applies at five points: when the two observations of a preparation disagree (no attempt exists yet); when the service refuses the push intent because the target differs from the expected one; when the observation before the push shows a target that does not hold the pushed commit; when the lease-protected push is refused and a new observation shows a moved target that does not hold the pushed commit; and, for the final confirmation, by observing again when the target moved on but still holds the commit being confirmed (up to three observations; a target that no longer holds it is an unresolved integration as before). A push that fails with an unmoved target keeps its error. A command makes at most three attempts and one `operation_id` at most 32. When the last attempt was given up because the target moved, the command fails with a message that says the target kept changing and that the same `operation_id` can be retried, which then continues with a new attempt. Other reasons (a released or lost claim, a changed process identity, a changed scope, a lost merge ref) keep the earlier unresolved-integration error. History rewrites that remove already integrated commits are not tolerated; the integration and the completion check refuse them.

## Recovery and completion

The private action journal freezes the operation ID, actor execution, options, exact request bodies and keys before effects. Repeating an ID with different material is rejected. A completed operation replays its historical service result even after task handoff or a later target generation; it grants no new write. A restarted command audits and releases its previous owned reservation before obtaining a fresh target execution. An uncertain release response replays the saved release operation. An expired reservation may be explicitly replaced, with the old intent retained in history.

The independent worker renews the target for the command's recorded PID, kernel start identity and boot identity. It never reports that command as a task agent or sends task requests to it. Only an answer of the server that ends the execution (`taken_over`, a lost or replaced target, an integration `revision_conflict`) stops the recorded command; a transport failure is retried without stopping it. The CLI cancels its owned Git process tree on a termination signal. Uncommitted task worktrees are preserved.

A final receipt is indexed by task and generation. `done` requires that receipt to match the current closed review target. Explicit no-Git plans with target `N/A` and no formal delivery retain their existing completion semantics. Portable wrap-up evidence names the integration ID, execution and generation; the service checks its immutable result, source, target and member generation. Clients fetch the source ref and verify that the actual remote still contains the published target. For a `replayed` receipt the client also repeats the commit-by-commit comparison of the recorded ranges and requires `R` instead of `C` in the target; for a `via` receipt it requires `via_commit` as well. A missing source/delivery ref or a rewound target refuses completion despite cached objects. Historical records without the new optional fields retain their bytes.

The service checks a wrap-up dispatch's integration identity when the dispatch is prepared, attempted, reauthorized or authorized for wrap-up, so each of those needs an integration of the holder's own generation. Ending a dispatch with `end` (failed or cancelled) and settling a completed one only release the card's binding and consume no integration evidence, so they skip that check. After a takeover that retained an accepted wrap-up round of an earlier generation, the new holder ends the round with a decision, publishes its own final integration, and prepares a new wrap-up round on it.

The evidence is built from the public receipt: `id` is `integration_id`, `request.execution_id` is `integration_execution`, `generation` is `integration_generation`, `request.commit` is `source_commit`, `observed_target` is `target_commit`, and `request.target_ref` is `target_ref`. Before it reads the execution, the client reports each missing identity field by name with that mapping. After it reads the named execution receipt, it reports each differing value with the receipt value. Both checks run before any request or dispatch is created, so neither looks like an unresolved integration. The released rules carry the complete JSON example in `rules/snippets/commands/dispatch.md` "Distributed wrap-up evidence".

Completion is verified by the client. The service checks its published receipt and never runs Git, so it does not depend on its working directory or on a Git installation. The complete binary links the client's Git verifier for local boards (`board.SetWrapUpGitVerifier` from `internal/launch`); the service's move passes `board.MoveOptions.ServiceCompletion`, which keeps that process-wide verifier out of the service path. The client verifies in the recorded execution worktree, or, when cleanup already removed it, in the current directory when its `origin` and push URL are the registered project remote. Another project's clone and a checkout that cannot fetch the evidence refuse completion.

Delivery refs (`refs/heads/kander/<project>/<task>/g<generation>/<commit>`, which also hold start baselines) and integration source refs (`refs/heads/kander/<project>/integrations/<execution>/<commit>`) are evidence with their own lifecycle. They never change while a reader may need them: integration, a takeover on another host, wrap-up verification and the first Git verification of a completed wrap-up by `kander coordinator reconcile` fetch them. Task and group branch cleanup never deletes them, and agents never delete them by hand.

The client deletes them in the completion cleanup, after the card or group has no reader left; the server runs no Git, so it only answers which refs go:

- A standalone card, after `kander move <task-id> done` succeeds (a replayed bound move included): every ref below `refs/heads/kander/<project>/<task>/` that has the exact `DeliveryRef` form, for every generation, and the source ref of every integration execution whose request has no `group_id` and names only this card, retired and failed attempts and the re-integrations after a takeover included. `GET /projects/{p}/tasks/{t}/evidence-cleanup` answers with the card and those executions once it is in `done/`.
- A task group, once it is settled: every member is in `done/`, `archived/` or `trash/` (a removed member is no longer a member) and, when the group has a checkpoint, every completed wrap-up of a `done/` member has its `wrap-up-git/<dispatch-id>.json` record. In distributed mode the service's `move done` writes no such record (`ServiceCompletion`); the next reconcile verifies the wrap-up in Git, reading the deliveries of every member and the integration source, and then writes it. Both a member's `move done` and a `complete` reconcile ask `GET /projects/{p}/groups/{g}/evidence-cleanup` (a member's task route answers for its group) after their own commit. The service serializes those commits and each read follows its own, so the read after the commit that settles the group sees it settled, whichever of the two commits last; deleting twice is harmless. The cleanup deletes the task evidence of the `done/` members and the source refs of every integration execution with that `group_id` (`initialize`, `assemble`, `deliver`, retired and failed attempts included). `archived/` and `trash/` members keep theirs. A group that a non-trigger finished last (an archive, a trash move, a recovery) settles on the reconcile the rules ask for after the last member finished. A group whose checkpoint archive cleanup removed counts as a group without a checkpoint.

The client first checks that the checkout's `origin` and push URL are the registered remote (the recorded execution worktree, else the current directory) and that `origin` has no other push URL, because a push reaches every one; it then lists the owned refs with `git ls-remote`, deletes only listed refs with `git push --delete`, and lists again after a failed push: a ref the remote no longer lists counts as deleted. A cleanup that fails, a refused deletion, a network error or no usable checkout, only prints a warning naming the refs it kept and why (a ref whose deletion could not be checked is marked unconfirmed, and when the remote could not be listed at all the warning names the owned prefixes and source refs to check); the card stays in `done/`, and the move or reconcile keeps its output and exit status. Refs below `integrations/` are never deleted by prefix. After the cleanup a `replayed` receipt's original reviewed commits may be garbage collected by the remote and can no longer be fetched from it by SHA. Refs published before this cleanup existed, and refs left by a client that stopped before its cleanup, are not removed.

`kander coordinator integration-show <target-ref>` prints the public target record. REST routes under `/projects/{project}/integrations/{id}` provide reads, observations, claim, renewal, phase actions and historical execution results. `/projects/{project}/tasks/{task}/integration` reads that task's current-generation final result. `/projects/{project}/tasks/{task}/evidence-cleanup` and `/projects/{project}/groups/{group}/evidence-cleanup` read the cleanup scope above. Public responses never include the private bearer except the direct claim grant.
